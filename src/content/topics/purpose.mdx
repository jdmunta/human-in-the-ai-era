<Lede>
Meaning looks backward: it is the story that makes what happened cohere. Purpose
looks forward: it is the thing you are pointed at. They are constantly confused,
and the confusion matters, because automation affects them differently. Meaning
has to be *constructed*. Purpose has to be *selected* — and for almost all of
human history, nobody had to select it.
</Lede>

## Necessity used to do the choosing

Purpose was assigned by scarcity. The crop needed planting; you planted. The
child was ill; you sat up. The village needed a roof, a smith, a scribe, a
soldier. The question "what should I devote my life to?" is a luxury good, and
like most luxury goods it was, until recently, available to a tiny minority —
aristocrats, monastics, and a handful of scholars who found the freedom
bewildering enough to write books about it.

Industrial economies expanded that minority enormously but kept the assignment
mechanism intact: the labour market told you what was needed and paid you to
supply it. You could choose *among* assignments. You rarely had to invent one.

Machine cognition attacks the assignment mechanism directly. Not because there
is nothing left to do — there is a great deal left to do — but because the
things that obviously, urgently, undeniably need *you specifically* are getting
rarer. Purpose stops being issued and starts having to be authored.

<Claim label="The claim">
<p>
Freedom from assignment is not the same as purpose. Being unassigned is a
vacuum, and vacuums are unpleasant. The skill that the next era demands is not
finding your passion — a famously bad instruction — but *committing to a stake*:
picking something whose outcome you will actually feel, and then closing the
exits.
</p>
</Claim>

## Why "find your passion" fails

The advice fails for a structural reason. Passion is usually an *output* of
sustained engagement, not an input to it. People who love their work
overwhelmingly report that the love arrived after competence, not before it.
Telling an unassigned person to search their feelings for a pre-existing passion
sends them looking for something that is generated by the very process they have
not started.

<Sidenote>
This is the core finding behind self-determination theory: motivation deepens
when an activity supplies autonomy, competence and relatedness. Competence is
earned in the middle of the work, which is why the search-first strategy stalls.
</Sidenote>

Worse, in an era of infinite optionality, searching is nearly costless and
therefore never terminates. You can sample forever. The machine will happily
help you sample forever — it will generate you a hundred plausible life
directions before lunch. Optionality feels like wealth and behaves like debt.

## The stake test

A useful substitute for passion-hunting is a test with three questions. A
candidate purpose has to pass all three.

- **Consequence.** If this goes badly, do I feel it — in my time, money, standing
  or relationships? Purpose without downside is a hobby with good branding.
- **Particularity.** Does it need *me*, or would any competent agent do? You do
  not have to be irreplaceable. But if a model could hold the stake as well as
  you can, the stake is not doing its job.
- **Duration.** Can I still be at this in three years? Purposes that resolve in a
  weekend are projects. Projects are good. They are not purposes.

Notice that none of the three mention enjoyment. Enjoyment is a downstream
effect and an unreliable compass; consequence, particularity and duration are
things you can check on a Tuesday.

## Stewardship as the default shape

The most robust form of purpose available to most people is not invention or
achievement but **stewardship**: taking responsibility for something that would
degrade without attention. A body of work. A piece of land. An institution. A
craft tradition. A person. A body of knowledge. A neighbourhood.

Stewardship is robust because it is defined by an obligation rather than an
outcome, which means it cannot be completed and therefore cannot leave you
stranded afterwards. It also has an unusual property in the AI context: the
machine can help you steward enormously well, and doing so does not dilute the
purpose at all. Nobody feels their care for an ageing parent is diminished by
using a calendar.

<Counter label="The strongest objection">
<p>
"Chosen purpose is thinner than assigned purpose, and everyone knows it. A stake
you selected is a stake you can unselect at 2 a.m. when it stops being fun. The
reason assigned duties held people was precisely that they were not up for
renegotiation. Voluntary commitment is commitment with an escape hatch built in,
and escape hatches get used."
</p>
</Counter>

This is the real difficulty, and the answer is not a better argument but a
better architecture. Chosen commitments hold when they are made *structurally
costly to abandon*: made publicly, entangled with other people, given a
schedule, given money. Almost every durable human institution — marriage, tenure,
apprenticeship, monastic vows, partnership agreements — is a technology for
converting a choice into something closer to an assignment. We will need to
rebuild those technologies for a context where the market no longer supplies
them by default.

<Practices label="Practices">
  <Practice n="1" title="Run the stake test on what you already do">
    Take your three largest time commitments and score each on consequence,
    particularity and duration. Most people discover their biggest time sink
    fails all three, and something they treat as peripheral passes all three.
  </Practice>
  <Practice n="2" title="Close one exit">
    Take one commitment you keep half-making and make it expensive to leave: tell
    someone specific, put it on a recurring calendar, pay for it, or partner with
    a person who will notice your absence.
  </Practice>
  <Practice n="3" title="Declare a stewardship">
    Name one thing you are the custodian of. Write down what happens to it if you
    stop. If nothing happens, you have not found it yet.
  </Practice>
  <Practice n="4" title="Set an optionality budget">
    Decide in advance how long you will explore before committing — a season, not
    a feeling. Infinite sampling is the failure mode the tools now actively
    encourage.
  </Practice>
</Practices>
